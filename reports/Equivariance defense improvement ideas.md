# Measure pixels, attack harder, claim narrowly

The best additions to this FYP are mostly not new defense mechanisms. They are a few cheap, clearly novel *analyses* plus a much harder *evaluation*. The single highest-value novel addition is a **pixel-level AUROC study of the dense equivariance error**: how well it separates attack-flipped pixels from clean ones, across transformation families (flip, rescale, random-offset crop, ColorJitter) and error metrics (L2, KL, flip-rate, feature cosine). No prior paper reports this, it needs only forward passes, and the per-pixel budget ε(p) claim depends on it. The second is **random-offset crop views**, borrowed from Xiao et al.'s spatial-consistency detector, which stayed near 100% AUC under an adaptive attack. These views make the gate and ε(p) harder to evade. The third is a **calibrated ε(p)** that fuses equivariance error with output entropy, tested against an entropy-only and an attention-style budget. Without those ablations the claim that *equivariance* error is the right per-pixel signal cannot be defended. Several planned claims need narrowing. The gate is not novel (Mao's own appendix and DGAP). View averaging is not novel (TTE, Lee & Kim). The momentum optimiser is a cited component. The planned "full-gradient" attack is nearly identical to BPDA because the defense uses `sign()`. The BPDA weakness is new only for the adversarially trained model, since Mao already reported 11.64 mIoU under BPDA for the clean model. Restart selection, diffusion priors, MAE heads, certification and new datasets should be dropped: each is either already broken in the literature or too costly for one GPU and four weeks. The prioritised plan at the end fits about 20 working days. The phase-ordered core (P0–P6) has to start immediately, because no phase has begun.

## The per-pixel budget claim survives, but only in narrow form

Across four literature sweeps, no work was found that uses a dense equivariance or consistency error map to set a per-pixel purification budget in a test-time defense for semantic segmentation. The closest works differ on at least one axis. Pei et al.'s "Divide and Conquer" injects stronger diffusion noise on classifier-attended pixels (τ≈0.8 attention mask, x(t_l)⊙M + x(t_s)⊙(1−M)). It covers **classification only** and reports 59.38% PGD+EOT on CIFAR-10 ([arXiv 2503.01407](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01407)). SuperPure masks pixels by super-resolution reconstruction error, but it targets patch attacks on classifiers ([arXiv 2505.16318](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16318)). SSNI sets a **per-sample** diffusion noise level from score norms ([arXiv 2506.06027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06027)), and UAD-RS chooses noise adaptively per image, including for remote-sensing segmentation ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1569843224004850)). The defensible wording is therefore: *"to our knowledge, the first test-time defense for semantic segmentation that uses the dense equivariance error to set a per-pixel ℓ∞ purification budget."* The write-up should cite Pei, SuperPure, SSNI, UAD-RS and pixel-reweighted adversarial training as the nearest neighbours and state the differences. Each sweep was limited to about 15–17 tool calls, and Semantic Scholar's citation API was rate-limited. A final Scholar query ("spatially varying epsilon purification", "pixel-wise test-time segmentation purification") close to submission is therefore mandatory, not optional.

The image-level gate is a different matter, and the plan should stop presenting it as novel. Mao et al.'s Appendix A.3.1 already proposes a **detect-then-defend threshold on the equivariance score** to preserve clean accuracy. Their score drops from **0.694 clean to 0.448 attacked** on Cityscapes ([ar5iv 2212.06079](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.06079)). DGAP applies the same gate-plus-adaptive-strength design to audio, passing low-shift inputs through unchanged ([arXiv 2610.10752](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10752v1)). Image-level detection on Cityscapes is also easy for non-adaptive attacks: Xiao et al.'s spatial-consistency detector reaches **100% AUC with K≥5 patch pairs** on DRN ([ar5iv 1810.05162](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.05162)). A gate AUROC near 1 on PGD-50 will impress nobody. The gate should be framed as an efficiency and clean-accuracy device. The evidence that matters is the gate's TPR/FPR *under the gate-aware attack*, following Carlini & Wagner's demonstration that all ten detectors they tested fell to adaptive losses ([arXiv 1705.07263](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.07263)).

The supporting components should be stated as cited engineering. Test-time transformation ensembling (TTE) is direct prior art for view averaging ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021W/AROW/papers/Perez_Enhancing_Adversarial_Robustness_via_Test-Time_Transformation_Ensembling_ICCVW_2021_paper.pdf)). So are Hill et al.'s EOT defense ([arXiv 2005.13525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13525v2)) and Lee & Kim's 10-run purification ensemble ([ar5iv 2303.09051](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.09051)). The momentum and step-decay optimiser is an APGD/MI-FGSM swap ([arXiv 2003.01690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.01690)). What *is* worth reporting is the empirical finding behind it: the 20-step defense collapses to about one effective step because injected noise swamps the normalised gradient. One caution comes from the diffusion literature: much of a purifier's robustness can come from randomness rather than optimisation. DiffPure falls from 69.04% to 55.96% when going from EOT-1 to EOT-10 ([arXiv 2404.14309](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.14309)). A cleaner optimiser can therefore raise PGD numbers and *lower* EOT numbers, and both must be reported.

## Three cheap ideas turn the error map into evidence

**Pixel-level separability, per transformation and per metric (1–3 days, forward passes only).** No paper was found that reports pixel-level AUROC of an equivariance or consistency map for attacked versus clean pixels in segmentation. Maag et al.'s uncertainty detector, the closest dense work, aggregates entropy, variation ratio and margin to image level. It shows heatmaps only qualitatively and has no adaptive evaluation ([arXiv 2408.10021](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.10021v1)). Mao's defense uses feature-level equivariance, but their detection appendix uses output-level variance, and they do no layer ablation ([ar5iv 2212.06079](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.06079)). The proposed table crosses {L2 on softmax, symmetric KL, label-flip rate, penultimate-feature cosine} with {flip, rescale, random crop, ColorJitter} and reports pixel AUROC on tune100. It also covers PGD, SegPGD and a low-ε attack, because untargeted iterative attacks are the hardest to detect in Maag's study. One methodological choice must be stated explicitly. A global ℓ∞ attack perturbs every pixel, so the positive label has to be **"pixels whose prediction the attack flipped"**. This table doubles as the P2 go/no-go. If pixel AUROC is weak but image AUROC is strong, the fallback is a two-level budget (Pei-style split) or a gate only. DRAM warns that self-supervised losses are "not sensitive enough to detect adversarial attacks accurately", so this outcome is a real possibility, not a formality ([arXiv 2303.12848](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.12848)).

**Random-offset crop views (2–3 days).** Xiao et al. showed which kinds of consistency check survive an attacker. Their random-offset patch consistency (512×512 crops with 32–64 px offsets) kept **92–100% AUC under an adaptive attack** that targeted the defender's own patches. Their Gaussian-blur consistency fell from 100% to **0% AUC** once attacked ([ar5iv 1810.05162](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.05162)). Tian et al. found the same pattern for classification: randomising the transformation retained about 70% white-box detection ([AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11828)). Crops are invertible on their overlap, so they fit the existing equivariance machinery: the error is computed on overlapping pixels only. They also enlarge the view space that a gate-aware attacker must cover with EOT. Mao's ablation bounds the transformation set, ranking resize (26.00) > flip (20.50) > small rotation (17.03) ≫ rotations ≥90° (8.61) ([ar5iv 2212.06079](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.06079)). Crops therefore add a cheap, well-motivated family without introducing transforms the model cannot handle. Randomness is not free robustness, though. It must be attacked with EOT and with fixed-seed attacks, as discussed below.

**A calibrated, ablated ε(p) (2–3 days).** No established method exists for turning a dense consistency map into a per-pixel budget. A defensible default maps each pixel's error to a percentile rank r(p) against the clean tune100 distribution. It then sets ε(p) = ε_min + (ε_max − ε_min)·σ((r(p) − τ)/T), smoothed with a 9–17 px kernel to match DRN's 8× stride, with only τ and T tuned. A two-feature per-pixel logistic regression (equivariance error plus entropy, fitted on tune100) gives a calibrated probability at almost no extra cost, because entropy is free. It also positions the work directly against the entropy-based segmentation detector ([arXiv 2305.12825](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.12825)). The essential companion is an ablation that swaps the signal while keeping everything else fixed: uniform ε, an entropy-only map, an attention/gradient-saliency map (the Pei-style signal), and the equivariance map. Only that comparison can support the word "equivariance-guided" in the title. A soft image-level gate in the style of SSNI, mapping mean error to a global ε through a monotone function, costs about a day. It gives a cleaner ablation than a hard threshold, and it is easier to attack honestly with a differentiable surrogate.

## Credibility depends on the attack suite, and the full-gradient attack hides a trap

The literature is unforgiving on this point. Croce et al. re-evaluated adaptive test-time defenses and found that **"none significantly improve upon static defenses"**. SOAP fell from 51.02% to **3.7%**, and Mao's own 2021 reversal defense fell from 63.83% to 58.4%, below its 59.4% static model, at 407× cost ([ar5iv 2202.13711](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.13711)). The project's own reproduction already shows this pattern on the adversarially trained model: **BPDA+defense 22.55 mIoU is below undefended PGD-50 at 25.26**, whereas Mao reported 29.83 under BPDA ([ar5iv 2212.06079](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.06079)). This is the finding to claim, and the citation sweep found no third-party adaptive evaluation of Mao 2023. OpenAlex lists only three citing works, none of which attacks it ([OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Robust%20Perception%20through%20Equivariance)). The claim must be narrowed, though. For the clean model, Mao themselves report the collapse (PGD 30.76 → **BPDA 11.64**), so "weaker against BPDA than claimed" applies to the Table 1 adversarially trained setting and to the paper's PGD-level headline, not to every number in the paper.

Five low-cost attack additions close most of the gap to Croce's six-point checklist. **BPDA through intermediate iterates** averages the attack gradient over predictions on all purifier iterates. It is how SOAP was broken, and it costs nothing extra because the loop already produces those iterates. **Transfer of unsuccessful perturbations** from the undefended model is nearly free and has beaten naive AutoAttack on several defenses. **Fixed-seed attacks** freeze the RNG and attack a now-deterministic defense; for Mao 2021 this beat 16-sample EOT (56.4% vs 58.4%) ([ar5iv 2202.13711](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.13711)). **N-evaluation** reports the worst case over several resubmissions to the stochastic defense, as DiffHammer recommends ([NeurIPS 2024](https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2024/wang2024neurips-diffhammer)). **PGD+EOT should be preferred over AutoAttack**, which overstated DiffPure's robustness at 63.60% against 46.84% under PGD+EOT ([ar5iv 2303.09051](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.09051)). On the segmentation side, SegPGD alone is no longer enough. SEA (APGD over masked CE, balanced masked CE and Jensen-Shannon losses, with radius reduction) showed that SegPGD and CosPGD overestimate mIoU robustness by more than 10 points ([arXiv 2306.12941](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.12941)). CosPGD is a few lines of code and drives DeepLabV3 to about 0% where SegPGD leaves 2.69% ([arXiv 2302.02213](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.02213v3)). A "SEA-lite" (100 iterations per loss, run through BPDA on test100) is affordable and would be the first SEA-style evaluation found for any test-time segmentation defense.

The trap is the planned full-gradient attack. The defense update is x_{t+1} = Π(x_t − η·sign(∇L_eq + noise)), and ∂sign/∂x = 0 almost everywhere. Exact autograd through the unrolled defense therefore reduces to the identity times projection and clip masks: **the "full gradient" is BPDA in disguise**. An examiner can reasonably call that gradient masking. This step is a calculus argument from the notes, not a published result. The fix is to show it empirically: the cosine between the unrolled and BPDA gradients should be about 1. Then add a **soft-surrogate full-gradient attack** that replaces sign with tanh(g/τ) or g/‖g‖∞ and double-backpropagates. Lee & Kim found that such surrogate processes beat both the adjoint method and BPDA; for GDMP, BPDA reported 75.59% where the surrogate gave 24.06% ([ar5iv 2303.09051](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.09051)). Memory is the constraint. Unrolling 20 steps × 7–9 views at 680×340 with `create_graph=True` likely needs tens of GB (estimated, not measured). A **2–5 step surrogate with proportionally larger step size** is both feasible and faithful, because the measured defense acts like about one effective step. Run it on 20–50 images. Croce attacked a 20-step defense with a 5-step version, so this compromise is accepted practice ([ar5iv 2202.13711](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.13711)).

The gate-aware attack must target both failure modes of the new design. In mode (a) the attacker evades the gate, keeping mean error ē below τ through a sigmoid surrogate of the threshold, so the undefended model is fooled outright. In mode (b) the attacker lets the gate fire but keeps the *local* error low where it attacks, which shrinks ε(p) exactly where the perturbation lives. Both use L = −CE + λ·max(0, ē − τ + κ) with gradients flowing *through* the error map: the stop-gradient is the defender's choice, not the attacker's. Sweep λ ∈ {0.1…1000}, use EOT over the defender's random crop views, and plot attack mIoU against ē. That plot tests Mao's "lose-lose" claim, that lowering equivariance error weakens the attack, at the point where it matters most. Mao's own adaptive term barely moved robust accuracy (about −0.2 points at λ_e=1000) ([ar5iv 2212.06079](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.06079)), but its sign could not be verified from the extraction, so re-derive it before citing it. No published attack on a per-pixel-budgeted purifier exists, so this evaluation is itself a contribution.

Two of the cuts need justification for an examiner. Restart selection, which picks the lowest-loss of several purifications, looks attractive given the one-step collapse. But SOAP already selected among 11 ε values by lowest self-supervised loss and was broken. The argmin also hands the attacker a target: drive one candidate to low equivariance error with wrong labels ([ar5iv 2202.13711](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.13711)). Diffusion purification has no small Cityscapes-resolution prior. Its evaluation is notoriously expensive: one Lee & Kim PGD-200/EOT-20 experiment took nearly 10 days on an A100 ([ar5iv 2303.09051](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.09051)). It would also turn the project into a different paper.

## A 20-working-day plan that front-loads the go/no-go

**Days 1–4 (P0–P2):** build `equi_lib/` and the attack cache (PGD-50, SegPGD and CosPGD adversarials for tune100/test100 on both checkpoints). Verify `EquiDefense.error_map()` against upstream. Run the pixel-AUROC × transform × metric table with random-offset crops included, plus the entropy baseline. This is the go/no-go: if pixel AUROC is weak, switch ε(p) to a two-level budget or gate-only and reframe the contribution around the evaluation. **Days 5–8 (P3):** profile first, then add momentum + step decay and noise scaled to gradient norm, and sweep steps on tune100. Produce the compute–robustness Pareto as a by-product. Run the sign-gradient cosine check now, because it decides the shape of the full-gradient attack. **Days 9–12 (P4–P5):** add the calibrated ε(p) and the soft and hard gates. Run the four-signal ablation and add view averaging. **Days 13–17 (P6):** run BPDA+EOT and BPDA-iterates, fixed-seed, transfer, the soft-surrogate T′=2–5 attack on 20–50 images, gate-aware modes (a) and (b) with the λ sweep, and SEA-lite via BPDA. Use 3 seeds, worst case over attacks per image, and N-evaluation. **Days 18–20 (P7):** full500 for the final configuration and the strongest attack, the Gradio demo, and the write-up. Abstention, the ε sweep and corruptions go in only if P6 finishes early. Tune only on tune100, report on test100/full500, and keep every new number in `results/improvements/`.

## Conclusion

The likely outcome needs stating plainly. Under BPDA-iterates, fixed-seed and gate-aware attacks, the gated ε(p) defense may land near the static model on the adversarially trained checkpoint, as almost every test-time defense in Croce's study did. That is not a failed project. A per-pixel separability study that nobody has published, a novel defense evaluated with attacks designed specifically against it, and an independent demonstration that the Table 1 robustness does not survive BPDA together make a stronger and more honest FYP than a PGD-only improvement would. The grade then depends less on beating 29.65 mIoU than on whether the minimum over attacks is reported as the headline.

The one decision to make early is how to read the pixel-AUROC table on day 4. If equivariance error clearly beats entropy at pixel level, ε(p) is the paper and the rest supports it. If it does not, the same experiments still yield a publishable negative result about dense self-supervised signals, and the evaluation suite becomes the main contribution. Committing to the go/no-go threshold in writing before seeing the numbers protects the project from tuning toward the claim.
